# 01 Product Form and User Experience

## 1. Who the product serves and what it solves

PeptiCraft addresses a specific gap. For people who want to make "protein materials with biological function", the point of blockage has never been a lack of inspiration, but rather three separate obstacles.

The first obstacle is **tool fragmentation**. Designing a functional peptide means simultaneously facing a string of mutually independent prediction tools: antioxidant activity is predicted by AnOxPePred, toxicity by ToxinPred3, hemolysis (the degree to which red blood cells are destroyed) by HemoPI2, immunogenicity (predicting whether this sequence will be recognized by the immune system as foreign and thereby trigger an antibody or T-cell response) by MHCflurry, thermal stability by TemStaPro, soluble expression by SoDoPE, and cell-penetrating ability (whether a peptide can cross the cell membrane on its own, which determines whether it can actually enter the skin when applied topically) by pLM4CPPs. These tools differ in input format, output scale, and decision threshold; scripts must be written to string them together, and failed samples caused by repeated sequences must also be handled. For teams without a computational background, this is itself an impassable threshold.

The second obstacle is that **safety has been wrongly treated as a score**. The conventional practice folds toxicity, hemolysis, and immunogenicity into a single weighted total for ranking, so that a peptide with an extremely high antioxidant score can, even with a clearly excessive hemolytic index, pull the total up with its high scores and remain on the candidate list. The real problem surfaces only at the wet-lab stage, and the cost of one wet-lab round is several weeks of time and a budget. The third obstacle is that **results are unreadable**. Prediction tools deliver a pile of decimals between 0 and 1 without a frame of reference. Given the number 0.64, one cannot judge whether it means the candidate can proceed or is close to danger, still less which experiment the score corresponds to.

The platform's answers to these three obstacles are respectively **integration, veto, and explanation**. Integration gathers a dozen or so tools into one pipeline, none of which the user touches; veto changes safety from a weighted term to a hard threshold, where any out-of-limit item is eliminated immediately; explanation makes every number appear together with its threshold, direction, and verdict, accompanied by a passage from which a conclusion can be read.

The user profiles thus divide into three classes, whose common points are a clear end-product form and an absence of protein-engineering capability. **Students and competition teams** have a definite project topic and delivery milestone, need to obtain a real, discussable candidate molecule within limited time, and lack the computing power to run these models. **Early-stage researchers** often have already settled the application direction and need a set of theoretically grounded candidates before formally starting experiments, to convince themselves that this path is worth taking; they care whether the provenance of the candidates is traceable and whether the parameters are transparent. **Independent innovators and small-brand R&D** have the product form in hand — masks, serums, hair masks, injectable fillers — and need to make "adding a functional ingredient" well grounded, while their budget does not allow them to pay a full set of computation and validation costs for a single attempt.

## 2. Product form: five routes of a single-page application

The product is a single-page web application with an all-English interface, composed of five routes, three of which are the primary working surfaces.

The **landing page** takes on the responsibility of explaining what the platform is. From top to bottom it presents the hero visual, four key figures, three module cards, five application route cards, three core principles, a round-by-round explanation of the screening pipeline, and status cards for the four functional directions. All four key figures are derived from the API rather than hard-coded: the search space takes the maximum, across the directions, of the number of peptides covered by the tools; the total construct count takes the sum over the directions of the construct counts; and the scaffold count and the direction count take the lengths of the scaffold list and the direction list, respectively. The page body still contains one hard-coded piece of copy reading "about twenty million peptides"; this order of magnitude agrees with the measured 20,248,885, but it is copy rather than a fetched value.

The **peptide library browser** is the entry point for viewing completed work. It is divided into three tabs: functional peptides and constructs, scaffolds, and linkers, with the tab counts coming from the totals returned by the API. The constructs tab offers two kinds of filtering — a label group by functional direction, and route buttons for "screened under which route" — and displays for each entry its rank, direction, status, peptide sequence and length, scaffold name with its binding-source marker, linker name, safety verdict color dot, composite score, and the functional score for that direction; next to the functional score it indicates whether that score can be used only for ranking. The page fetches the details only when an entry is expanded, rendering them with the same safety gate panel and tool score grid, which share a single component with the results page, so the thresholds and verdicts in the two places cannot drift.

The **builder** is the core of the product, guiding the user through the choice in five steps. In step one the application route is selected; the interface simultaneously lists which scaffolds that route accepts, the values of the three weight tiers, the immunogenicity threshold, and the additional non-veto check items specific to that route; after the route is selected, the first scaffold under that route is selected automatically. In step two the functional directions are multi-selected; directions whose status is empty cannot be selected, and the interface separately explains that the directions are independent of one another. In step three the scaffold is confirmed; candidates come from the selected route and can be further filtered by material form, and the interface is split into two tabs, "matching" and "all"; this step carries an explicit note stating that the scaffold binding of all current constructs is a placeholder value, that choosing a scaffold will not narrow the candidate set — which peptides pair with a given scaffold does not change on that account — but that it does determine the sequence each candidate is assembled into. In step four the linker is confirmed; all entries in the reference library are listed, each with its name, residue length, rigidity tier, sequence, and literature provenance; this step is not narrowed by route, because the linker library has not yet been mapped to application routes. In step five the results are given; candidates are listed in descending order of composite score, each carrying the fusion sequence and fusion length assembled with the selected scaffold and linker, and the counts of the various safety verdicts are tallied; the tally covers all candidates under that filter, not merely the portion displayed on the page. From any candidate in the results one can jump to the results page, with the pair of scaffold and linker used at that moment carried along, and on that page one can still switch to others and compare again. From walking the five steps to seeing candidates, the user makes five clicks.

The **results page** is where a single candidate is read, organized around four tabs. The construct tab is the main view: at the top are three blocks of metrics — composite score, rank, and aggregation propensity; below is the safety gate panel; below that the segment-colored sequence — scaffold in blue, linker in green, functional peptide in orange, with the functional peptide segment in bold, and the segment whose sequence is unknown shown in gray with a label rather than having residues drawn — then the side-by-side comparison table within the direction and six passages of interpretation text. The functional peptide tab shows the peptide's provenance, length, the nine per-tool scores, and a passage of locally computed sequence properties (molecular weight, net charge, cysteine count, and the proportion of aromatic and hydrophobic residues). The scaffold tab shows the current binding and the alternative scaffolds, and clicking an alternative immediately changes the query parameters to view this candidate again; scaffolds without a sequence cannot be clicked. The linker tab shows the unit composition and literature provenance of that linker. On the right side of the page there is also a fixed chat sidebar, and at the bottom a further input bar; both share the same chat endpoint, and a question about any metric receives an answer based on that construct's real scores.

The **scaffold detail page** unfolds the full picture of a single scaffold along five routes: basic information, provenance and patents, patented sequence variants, which application routes adopt it, experimental evidence, regulatory registration status, and other scaffolds in the library. The page has an explicit notice area reading "the following fields do not exist in this dataset", leaving fields that the dataset genuinely lacks empty rather than rendering them as blank cards — so that a reader can distinguish "this data does not exist" from "this part has broken down".

## 3. A walkthrough of one complete use

Consider a small team making medical dressings, whose goal is to add antioxidant function to a wound dressing in order to alleviate oxidative stress; they do not know from which peptide antioxidant capacity should come, nor which protein is suitable for making a fibrous dressing.

The first action upon entering the builder is to select the application route "wound dressing". After the selection, two things are done automatically by the platform: the delivery method is bound to the contact interface of that route, without user involvement; and the available scaffolds are narrowed to the four that route accepts — Giant Biogene FHLC, Trautec COL17, Yusong SF-4/SF-10, and Wuhan TRHCIII-1. At the same time the interface tells the user that for this route the immunogenicity threshold is tightened to 0.35, stricter than the 0.50 for mask patches and film-forming coatings, on the grounds that the wound barrier is already broken and the construct comes into direct contact with tissue fluid.

In step two the antioxidant direction is checked. In step three one of the four scaffolds is confirmed, or all candidates are kept to proceed to the next step. In step four a linker is chosen; here the interface explains the meaning of this choice: flexible units let the functional peptide swing freely on the scaffold surface, rigid units hold it away from the surface, both are legitimate, and which to use depends on whether the functional peptide needs unobstructed access to its target. Like the scaffold, it does not change the candidate list or any score, but changes the sequence assembled in the next step. In step five the build button is clicked; the page returns the list of candidates under that filter sorted by composite score, each carrying a fusion sequence assembled from scaffold, linker, and functional peptide segments, and reports how many of these candidates fully pass the safety verdict, how many sit on the line, and how many are vetoed. These counts are tallied over all candidates under the filter, so even when the page is truncated to the first few entries, these numbers still describe the entire candidate set.

After selecting a candidate and entering the results page, the user's reading order is: first check whether the safety verdict at the top passes entirely; then look at the composite score and the threshold verdict for each of the nine scores; then scroll the sequence downward to see clearly how the scaffold, linker, and functional peptide segments are joined; finally read the interpretation text, focusing on the expression and processing recommendations and the risk notes. If there is a question about some metric, one asks directly in the chat column on the right — why a certain score has this value, whether this candidate would still hold under another application route, or for a comparison of the trade-offs among several candidates under the selected route. In the end three things are to be taken away: the complete construct sequence, the corresponding expression plan, and a list of validations that still need to be performed.

## 4. Several design decisions that affect the experience

**Not having users answer questions they cannot answer.** Non-expert users simply cannot answer "which scaffold protein should be used", and expert users are also unwilling to be asked at every step. The platform therefore changes scaffold choice from a question requiring a user decision into a conclusion derived from the scenario: the user selects the product form, and the platform works backward to the scaffold. The same principle applied to different objects does not yield the same conclusion. Scaffolds are eight named proteins, and judging them requires knowledge of each protein's mechanical properties and evidence grade, so they can only be derived backward from the scenario. Linkers are not of this shape: the real difference among entries in the library is the rigidity tier; a flexible unit lets the functional peptide swing freely on the scaffold surface, a rigid unit holds it away from the surface, and this trade-off can be understood without protein-engineering training, so a single linker is opened up as a changeable option. It agrees with scaffolds on two points — both enter assembly, and neither changes the candidate list or the scores; and differs on one point — it is not derived from the scenario; the interface lists the whole library and by default stops at the first entry, and the user obtains a usable result without touching it. What stays in the background is the combinatorial enumeration over multiple sites, that is, exhausting the combinations of flexible and rigid units at one to five linker positions and then filtering them one by one; this is purely search work that the user can neither judge nor needs to judge.

**The primary classification directly reuses the labels already present in the dataset rather than inventing a new set.** The five application routes correspond to the five application label columns in the scaffold dataset; this is not a matter of convenience, but because three conditions hold simultaneously: every selectable node can filter out at least one scaffold, otherwise the user would click into an empty set; every scaffold can be filtered out by at least one node, otherwise it would be unreachable in the library; and no two nodes can filter out exactly the same set of scaffolds, otherwise one of them is a redundant entry and the user has made an uninformative choice between two names. Measurement shows that the five routes filter out 2, 4, 2, 4, and 2 protein clusters respectively, all eight clusters are reachable, and no two routes point to the same set of scaffolds. These three conditions constitute the criteria for judging whether an entry structure holds, and also explain why the earlier self-invented scenario hierarchy was ultimately abandoned — its subclasses filtered out exactly the same scaffolds, so after making a choice the user could not perceive what had been filtered out.

**The delivery method is derived from the scenario, not checked by the user.** This decision is easily misread as interaction simplification, but the actual reason is the scoring convention: the delivery method substantially changes the ranking and retention of candidates; the route of injectable filling that enters tissue and the route of wound dressing with a broken barrier tighten the immunogenicity threshold to 0.35, and the same candidate that is marked as passing under a mask patch may be directly vetoed under these two routes. Allowing the user to check it at will would amount to letting the user decide, without understanding the consequences, which candidates count as passing. After binding it to the scenario, the user can still change it, but only through the meaningful act of changing the product form. The "application technique", originally presented as an independent layer — coating, manual spreading, spraying, contact with the wound, and the like — is accordingly downgraded to a string of descriptive notes accompanying the route; it neither filters scaffolds nor participates in scoring, and as a mandatory option it would only make the user perform an uninformative choice.

**Safety is given a verdict, not a score.** The safety panel presents not one composite safety score but the measured value, threshold, verdict, and evaluated-or-not for each item, finally summarized into one overall verdict. The overall verdict has four values rather than two: all pass; vetoed because at least one item is out of limit; no item out of limit but one item falling within 10% of its threshold in the marginal zone; and nothing evaluated, so no verdict can be given. The last value exists to avoid presenting "never measured" as "passed" — a rule that runs through the entire interface.

**"Not evaluated" is not equal to zero.** All nine scores are nullable at the data layer; a null indicates that no measurement exists for that item and is a different matter from "truly measured as zero". In the interface a null renders as not evaluated rather than 0.00, and no fail badge is attached. The weight of this convention is visible from the coverage alone: immunogenicity has two mutually independent pathways, one covering 117 constructs and the other 186, and after overlapping there remain **198 constructs where both are empty**. If these were rendered as a score of zero, nearly two hundred candidates reading "immunogenicity zero, fully safe" would appear in the interface, when in fact they have never been measured.

**Scores that are only rankable are not dressed up as probabilities.** The functional scores of the anti-inflammatory and anti-melanogenic directions come from a model that discriminates whether something "looks like a functional peptide", not from measuring anti-inflammatory or anti-melanogenic activity; the database explicitly notes on these rows that the score may be used only for ranking and must not be read as a probability. Therefore the scores of these two directions carry a ranking marker on both the list rows and the tool grid, use no probability coloring, and receive no pass or fail badge.

**Failure reasons are generated from the item actually out of limit.** A vetoed candidate displays in the list not a general "failed safety" but the item that is actually out of limit — it may be toxicity, it may be hemolysis, it may be immunogenicity. The handling recommendations for the three differ, and a general presentation would lead the user to take the wrong next step.

## 5. The actual completeness of the current user experience

It is necessary to distinguish clearly which content in the interface is real data and which is still provisional form; this is something a technical handoff document must state.

The parts already genuinely connected cover a wide range: the content of all five pages comes from the database, and every value in the lists and details can be traced to a specific table and field; the safety verdict is determined on the spot against the thresholds of the selected route; the composite score is recomputed on the spot using the route's weight tier, and the same candidate has different composite scores under the five routes; the six passages of interpretation text are generated by the server from that construct's real scores and thresholds, and every number can be found in the corresponding field of the same response; the chat column is also connected to the same server-side generator, returning answers based on real data rather than fixed copy.

The parts still in provisional form are also several, and of different natures. **Choosing a scaffold and choosing a linker do not change the set of candidate peptides**, because the scaffold binding of all 496 constructs is a placeholder value and the linker foreign keys all point to the first row of the sample table; the interface states this truthfully with one note in each place, rather than letting the user believe that the choice took effect. What these two items change is the sequence each candidate is assembled into and the fusion length reported by the interface. **The linker library has not yet been mapped to application routes**, so the five routes receive the same list in step 4; scaffolds were narrowed by route long ago, and linkers will only catch up once this dimension is filled in the library. **The fusion sequence is not output in full when no scaffold is specified**: when the details of a single construct are called, the scaffold segment is marked as sequence unavailable, and the page draws only the two segments that really exist, linker and functional peptide; whereas the result sequence obtained by walking the five steps in the builder is complete, because it is spliced from the scaffold and linker selected on the spot by the user, and the interface states that this is assembled from request parameters rather than produced by the pipeline.

There are also several rough points at the interaction level, which do not affect data correctness but affect the feel of use: the peptide library browser currently has filters only, without a search box or sorting controls, which does not match the description "supports search, filtering, and sorting" in the engineering documentation; "show more" in the list only increases the number of entries per request without real pagination, and this point will be noticeably slow when the total number of constructs is large; the application has only five explicit routes and no catch-all route, so visiting a nonexistent address lands on a blank page; two files that are no longer rendered also remain in the project, one a style sheet included with the site template and one an old pinned style file, the latter with no reference point anywhere in the project.

There are also two deliberate restraints in the interface, which are design rather than defects. **Two directions carry a pending sign-off marker**: anti-inflammatory and anti-melanogenic have data in the library but the pipeline has not confirmed them, and the interface uses status markers to distinguish them from the two ready directions rather than hiding them or disguising them as equally mature. **Material form is an extracted value**: the material form of a scaffold (lyophilized sponge, self-assembling hydrogel, injectable gel, film-forming, fiber, membrane sheet, solution formulation) is not a field in the dataset but a classification manually extracted from the two prose passages on product use and key results; the interface uses it for filtering, but this column itself still awaits final team confirmation.
