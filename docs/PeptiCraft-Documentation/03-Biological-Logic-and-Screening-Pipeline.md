# 03 Biological Logic and Screening Pipeline

> Chinese version: [中文版](03-Biological-Logic-and-Screening-Pipeline.cn.md)

## 1. Design Object: Three-Segment Assembly

A usable recombinant fusion protein is made of three segments, each of which carries a different biological function; without any one of them the entire chain does not hold.

**The functional peptide** is the segment that produces the activity. It is a short chain of amino acids joined end to end, and the length range of interest in this project is 1 to 30 residues, which falls in the short-peptide range usually meant in the literature. There are four reasons for choosing a short peptide rather than a complete active protein: short peptides can be made by solid-phase chemical synthesis, and cost rises steeply with length, so a peptide of around twenty residues is a routine order whereas one of hundreds of residues becomes a recombinant expression task; short peptides have a small molecular weight and penetrate tissue and cell membranes markedly better than large proteins, which is a necessary condition for applications that must contact skin or a wound surface; short peptides themselves are usually less immunogenic and are not readily recognized by the immune system as large-molecule foreign bodies; and finally, short peptides can be fused to other proteins by genetic engineering without an additional chemical coupling step. The countervailing cost is that a short peptide on its own has almost no structural stability, is rapidly degraded by proteases in body fluids, and can provide no mechanical performance at all — which is precisely why a scaffold is needed.

**The scaffold** is the segment that carries the functional peptide and turns the whole molecule into a usable material. It is chosen from natural structural proteins, and the library in this project includes three classes: recombinant humanized collagen, silk protein and spider silk protein. What these proteins share is the ability to form a stable higher-order structure spontaneously: in collagen, three chains wind around one another into a triple helix, held by the geometric constraint of the recurring "glycine–any residue–hydroxyproline" motif in the sequence; silk and spider silk proteins stack the recurring "glycine–glycine–any residue" short repeat units into β-sheet layers, and thereby gain exceptionally high tensile strength and toughness. The scaffold determines what form of product the whole fusion protein can ultimately be made into — solution, gel, lyophilized sponge, fibre or film — so it is not a passive carrier but the determining factor of product form. This is also the fundamental reason the product removes scaffold selection from the user and instead derives it backwards from the application route.

**The linker** is the small segment in the middle. It takes part in no biological activity, and its function is spatial: it lets the functional peptide and the scaffold each fold correctly without interfering with one another. Without it, with the functional peptide sitting directly on the scaffold surface, two kinds of failure readily occur — the functional peptide becomes buried by a scaffold domain so that its active site cannot reach the substrate, or the functional peptide interferes with scaffold folding and destroys the mechanical performance the scaffold is supposed to provide. Linkers fall into two classes by mechanical character: the **flexible linker** is typified by the repeated "glycine–glycine–glycine–glycine–serine" (four glycines plus one serine, commonly abbreviated GGGGS); glycine is the only amino acid without a side chain, so the peptide chain has almost no conformational constraint in this segment, while serine contributes water solubility, and the effect of the whole segment is to leave both ends free room to move. The **rigid linker** is typified by the repeated "glutamate–alanine–alanine–alanine–lysine" (EAAAK motif); glutamate carries a negative charge and lysine a positive charge, a salt bridge forms between them and locks the segment into an α helix, and the effect is to fix the distance between the two ends. The two are not interchangeable, and choosing the wrong one deprives the fusion protein of the properties it should have. It is also the only one of the three assembly parts that the user selects directly in the builder, and the reason lies in precisely the difference just described: flexible or rigid is a trade-off that the usage scenario can answer directly and that requires no protein-engineering knowledge, whereas the scaffold trade-off requires knowing the properties of eight specific proteins and can therefore only be derived backwards from the application route.

There is coupling among the three segments, and this is where the difficulty of the whole matter lies. Changing the functional peptide changes the hydrophobicity and charge distribution of the entire fusion protein and hence its aggregation propensity; changing the scaffold changes the chemical environment in which the functional peptide sits and hence its apparent activity; changing the linker changes the relative orientation of the two ends. This is therefore not three independent optimizations but a joint selection problem that requires enumerating part of the combination space. The pipeline's full enumeration over linkers, ranking-based pre-screening over functional peptides and narrowing of scaffolds by application route are exactly the decomposition of an intractable full space into three tractable sub-problems.

## 2. The Four Functional Directions and Their Technical Routes

The platform precomputes four functional directions. These four directions are not four arbitrary labels; each corresponds to a different class of biological activity and each depends on a different class of predictive model, and only by understanding this can one understand why the data structures and maturities of the four directions are not level.

**Antioxidant** deals with free-radical scavenging capacity. Under oxidative stress cells produce excess reactive oxygen species, which attack lipids, proteins and nucleic acids and are one of the common causes of delayed wound healing and material ageing. The model used for prediction is called AnOxPePred, which outputs two mutually independent branches: one predicts free-radical scavenging activity (the term "branch head" denotes the sub-network within a multi-task model dedicated to one class of output), the other predicts metal-ion chelation capacity — chelating transition-metal ions indirectly suppresses the hydroxyl radicals produced by their catalysis, which is the second route of the antioxidant mechanism. The library additionally holds a support-vector-machine classifier that serves as the basis for splitting the candidate pool at ranking time. This direction is the most mature of the four: the pipeline runs through in full, and all 170 constructs pass the safety gate.

**Antimicrobial** deals with the capacity to inhibit bacterial growth. The mainstream mechanism is that cationic antimicrobial peptides adsorb onto the negatively charged bacterial membrane surface, and at a sufficient concentration insert into and disrupt membrane integrity, causing bacterial contents to leak out; such peptides usually carry a net positive charge and have an amphipathic helical structure, which is why the top-ranked antimicrobial peptide sequences in the library (for example a 29-residue sequence rich in tryptophan and lysine) display a marked amphipathic character. The models used for prediction are the AMPlify framework combined with protein-language-model features, with an antimicrobial channel from iMFP-LG as a cross-check. The data for this direction comprises only 65 entries, of which the positive-selection channel has only 7, the thinnest positive-selection channel in the whole library — the reason being that the score distribution of the antimicrobial model is narrow, and candidates that both pass the high-quantile split and satisfy the safety gate are inherently few.

**Anti-inflammatory** deals with the capacity to suppress the inflammatory response. The model it uses comes from the multi-label framework of iMFP-LG, taking its score through the channel specifically corresponding to anti-inflammatory peptides. There is one technical limitation here that must be represented faithfully in both the data and the interface: what this class of multi-label model does is **judge whether a sequence resembles known functional peptides**, not **measure anti-inflammatory activity itself**. In other words, a high score means that the sequence lies close to known anti-inflammatory peptides in feature space, not that it has been experimentally confirmed to suppress inflammation. For this reason the library annotates this score, on this batch of data, as usable for ranking only and not as a probability, and the product likewise gives no pass-or-fail badge in these two directions. This direction has 154 constructs, all pending sign-off.

**Anti-melanogenic** deals with the capacity to inhibit melanin production and corresponds directly to whitening-type functional materials. Its target is usually tyrosinase, the rate-limiting enzyme on the melanin synthesis pathway; inhibiting its activity reduces melanin output. The predictive model used in this direction is a tyrosinase-inhibitory-peptide predictor, which is not the same technical route as the neural networks used in the other three directions, and this is one reason it is listed separately. This direction has 107 constructs, likewise all pending sign-off.

The differences in technical route among the four directions also have a consequence for the data: the functional scores of the four directions are stored in the same slot, but the tool keys used to retrieve them differ, and each of the four directions further has a varying number of dedicated tool scores. This is not a design defect; the four directions simply use different models and different units of measure.

## 3. Biological Sources and Selection Basis of the Scaffolds

The eight protein clusters in the library fall into three classes by biological source, and the distribution of uses across the three classes follows a clear pattern.

**Recombinant collagen** accounts for five clusters, comprising humanized type III collagen and its tandem functional-domain variants, a human type XVII collagen fragment, and collagen-like protein. Collagen is the principal extracellular-matrix component of the dermis; its natural triple-helical structure gives it both biocompatibility and mechanical support, and it can be progressively degraded through the body's collagen metabolic pathways. This class carries the great majority of the high-grade evidence in the library: four of the five clusters reach E5 (regulatory review and post-market safety data), because they correspond to real medical-device products that have already obtained registration certificates in China — including injectable collagen fillers, repair dressings, and products in lyophilized-fibre form. The product files them under three routes: injectable filling, sheet mask and wound dressing.

**Silk protein** accounts for one cluster, the short recombinant silk fibroin SF-4/SF-10. The β-sheet structure of silk fibroin gives high strength together with good film-forming and fibre-forming capability, along with degradability and low immunogenicity. The representative sequence here has only 62 residues, making it the shortest scaffold in the library, which indicates that it is a heavily truncated engineered fragment rather than a complete silk fibroin heavy chain. Its evidence grade is E2 (cell experiments), and it carries an explicit evidence limitation stating that there is no evidence of material carriage or of a complete silk fibroin fibre structure; its material form in the library is therefore classified as a solution-type formulation ingredient. The product files it under sheet mask and wound dressing.

**Spider silk protein** accounts for two clusters, Bolt Threads' recombinant spider silk protein 18B and AMSilk's engineered eADF4(C16). Spider silk is one of the natural fibres with the best known toughness, its mechanical performance arising from the alternation of β-sheet crystalline regions and amorphous regions stacked from highly repetitive short motifs. The evidence grade of 18B is E4 (human use or controlled clinical study), with material form classified as film-forming and solution; eADF4(C16) has only E1 (purified protein or physicochemical, cell-free experiments), with an evidence limitation stating that the evidence is mainly cell-free and ex vivo, and material form classified as fibre and film-forming. It is one of the only scaffolds filed under the hair-care route, and that route is itself the one with the weakest evidence of the five.

**Evidence grade needs to be understood correctly.** It is not a score of "completeness" but "the highest level that can be verified". A scaffold labelled E5 means that regulatory review or post-market safety data support its safety in some application; it does not mean that the new use for which it is employed on the platform has also been validated. Conversely, E1 does not mean the protein is useless, only that the publicly checkable evidence stops at the cell-free level. The evidence-limitation field recorded in the library exists precisely to record this gap.

The scaffold library also has one unresolved problem: the scaffold foreign keys of all 496 constructs in the product library point to a single placeholder entry, whose name comes from a deprecated early mock dataset and which is not among the eight genuine protein clusters listed above. The scaffold binding of these 496 constructs is therefore itself a placeholder artefact rather than a genuine selection result, and the fusion sequence cannot currently be output in full.

## 4. Enumeration and Filtering of Linkers

In the current implementation linkers are split into two layers, one specified by the user and one kept in the background. What the user can judge is the rigid-versus-flexible dimension: a flexible unit lets the functional peptide swing freely on the scaffold surface, a rigid unit props it away from the surface, and what changes is the degree to which the functional peptide is shielded by the scaffold — this trade-off can be understood without protein-engineering training, so the single linker is exposed as an option that can be changed, and stands alongside the scaffold as one of the two assembly targets. What is kept in the background is the combinatorial enumeration over multiple sites, which is search work rather than judgement work: the combination space of flexible and rigid units is expanded completely, a flexible or a rigid unit being placed at each of one to five sites, which totals **62 combinations** (two for one site, four for two sites, eight for three sites, sixteen for four sites, thirty-two for five sites; sixty-two in total). The pipeline filters these sixty-two one by one in the assembly round, and what the user sees is the conclusion after filtering, not a choice made within the combination space. The actual state of this round at present is that only placeholder values have been written; see the fourth round in Section 5.

Every enumerated combination must pass two checks before entering the candidates. The first is **aggregation propensity**, measured by the AGGRESCAN score. This tool assigns each amino acid an aggregation-propensity value at residue scale, then accumulates it over a sliding window, outputting an estimate of the propensity of the whole sequence to form amyloid or non-specific aggregates; lower values are better, and a value above 22 is judged prone to aggregation. Aggregation is the most common cause of failure in the expression and purification of recombinant proteins — once a protein forms inclusion bodies, the downstream activity assays cannot even begin — so this item is filtered at the enumeration stage, and eliminated combinations never appear on the results page. The second check is the **three safety gates**, which use the same set of judgements as the whole construct.

The results of enumeration and filtering are shown faithfully on the results page: how many combinations were enumerated in total, how many were removed for aggregation propensity, and how many were removed by the safety gates. This is not decorative information; it tells the user how much screening pressure the final few candidates emerged from, rather than leaving them as something that dropped out of a black box.

## 5. The Four-Round Screening Pipeline

The whole pipeline is a continuously converging funnel, run once for each of the four directions; the responsibility and measured duration of each round are as follows.

**The first round is functional ranking.** The direction's functional predictor scores and ranks all twenty million peptides in the library. There is a design detail here: the split does not take only the high-scoring end but simultaneously retains a control channel — the high-scoring end enters positive selection, and the lowest-scoring batch is retained as a control. The purpose of the control channel is to provide a baseline of "known to have poor activity", with which to verify that the scoring system really does discriminate good from bad, rather than performing a meaningless ranking within a very narrow dynamic range. This round has the largest computational load but a manageable duration, on the order of about half an hour, because the functional predictors are batch inference tasks.

**The second round is the safety hard-threshold screen.** Three veto items are checked simultaneously, and exceeding the limit on any one removes the candidate immediately, so that it takes no part in the subsequent weighting. This round is the bottleneck of the whole pipeline: inference by the toxicity predictor is single-threaded, and running it over twenty million peptides takes about twenty-four hours and cannot be interrupted. The measured duration in the library is consistent with this order of magnitude. This round is also where the design stance that "safety is a threshold, not a score" is most visible — passing is passing, and failing means not entering the candidate pool at all, with no compensation mechanism whatsoever.

**The third round is in-depth developability scoring.** Candidates that pass the safety gates are fed into several developability metrics, which are weighted into a composite score used for ranking. The specific weighting algorithm is set out in Section 7, and its core characteristic is that it uses no manually preset weights. This round takes on the order of about five hours.

**The fourth round is construct enumeration and assembly.** Top-ranked functional peptides are assembled with a scaffold and a linker into complete constructs and written to the database. This round turns "a functional peptide" into "a molecule that can be sent to the laboratory", and is also the source of the current 496 constructs. One step in this round is still a placeholder in the current implementation: the scaffold segment is not joined to a real sequence, and the script writes only the minimal viable combination of "peptide × placeholder scaffold × placeholder linker", so that the table structure and sample data are complete, leaving the real binding to the compute service.

## 6. Safety Gates: Where the Thresholds Come From

The safety determination consists of three veto items and two non-veto soft signals; the provenance of each threshold differs, and that difference is marked explicitly in both the data and the API.

**Toxicity** is predicted by ToxinPred3 with a threshold of 0.38, and the direction is lower-is-better (a lower score indicates less toxin-like). This value is not the tool's default; it was calibrated by the project after running its own library of twenty million peptides through the full library and observing the score distribution: the tool's default threshold would admit a batch of unacceptable candidates on this project's data distribution, and 0.38 suppresses both false negatives and false positives. It is the only value in the entire threshold system that belongs explicitly to a "project decision" rather than to a "tool recommendation" or a "route-specific convention".

**Hemolysis** is predicted by HemoPI2 with a threshold of 0.55, the direction likewise being lower-is-better. Hemolysis means destruction of red blood cells and release of haemoglobin, a risk that any material contacting blood or entering the body must exclude. It should be noted that at library level this tool covers only a 500,000-entry sample; although all 496 constructs have a value, its coverage is weaker than that of the other tools.

**Immunogenicity** is composed of two independent pathways, and this is the point most easily oversimplified. The **MHC-I pathway** is predicted by MHCflurry, which estimates the probability that a peptide segment is presented on the cell surface by MHC class I molecules, as a proxy for T-cell immunogenicity — the higher the presentation probability, the more likely the peptide is recognized and cleared by CD8-positive cytotoxic T cells. The default strong-binder boundary of this tool is around 0.50; the platform adopts 0.50 as the general threshold and tightens it to 0.35 under the wound-dressing and injectable-filling routes. The **MHC-II pathway** is predicted by NetMHCIIpan, whose output percentile rank measures the capacity of a peptide to be presented by MHC class II molecules and thereby to recruit CD4-positive helper T cells, and **a lower rank indicates stronger binding**, a rank in the top 2% being the usual boundary for a strong binder. The two pathways do not measure the same thing and cannot substitute for one another, nor can a percentile rank be divided by one hundred and passed off as a probability. The product outputs the two as separate fields: MHC-I takes part in the veto determination, while MHC-II is reported as a soft signal because its coverage is incomplete.

Coverage is the most practical limitation on this item: MHC-I has values on only 117 constructs, because the tool applies only to peptides of 5 to 15 residues and the length distribution in the library extends far beyond that interval; MHC-II has values on 186. At least one of the two has a value on 298 constructs, and **198 constructs have neither**. For these constructs the immunogenicity gate is in the "not assessed" state.

**Not assessed does not constitute elimination, and this is an explicit rule of the original pipeline**, written in the same place in each of the four direction proposals: a metric eliminates a candidate only when a measured value exists and falls outside the boundary; a missing metric eliminates nothing but must be reported as not assessed. The product follows this rule strictly, and the interface rendering "not assessed" as "not assessed" rather than "pass" is a direct consequence of it — describing something never measured as passing amounts to giving a safety assurance without grounds.

Two **non-veto soft signals** also need to be stated clearly. **B-cell epitope propensity** is predicted by BepiPred-3.0 and measures the likelihood of a peptide segment being recognized by antibodies, with a threshold of 0.1512; it affects the risk of generating neutralizing antibodies but does not directly trigger a T-cell immune response, so it is reported rather than vetoed. **Allergenicity** is predicted by AlgPred2 with a threshold of 0.3; it is a significant signal for topical materials and is likewise reported rather than vetoed — this must be distinguished from the veto items in the interface, since marking the whole safety group as veto gates would be wrong.

The safety-gate thresholds carry a provenance marker in the API that distinguishes three cases: **from the tool itself** (toxicity and hemolysis), **from the route's screening configuration** (the three immunogenicity tiers 0.35 / 0.50 / 0.60), and **from a platform constant** (the 2% boundary for a strong MHC-II binder, which is taken from the tool's customary usage; on the pipeline it was originally carried only as a measured value, with no threshold set). Distinguishing the three has practical consequences: adjusting a tool threshold affects all routes, whereas adjusting a route-specific convention affects only one route.

## 7. Composite Score: Weights Determined by the Data

The composite score is not a prediction but a derived ranking value, composed by weighting several developability components. Its algorithm comes from the third round of the pipeline, and its core design is that **it uses no manually preset weights**.

The procedure is as follows. First, each component is normalized within the candidate pool and its direction unified so that "larger is better" holds for all components — components where a larger value is worse (allergenicity, B-cell epitope propensity, aggregation propensity) are sign-flipped after normalization. Then a quantity called the **Winsorized standard deviation** is used as the source of each component's weight. "Standard deviation" measures the dispersion of a set of values; "Winsorized" means that extreme values are first clipped to the 5th and 95th percentiles before the calculation, so that a single outlier cannot dominate the weight of an entire component. The weights are normalized by the Winsorized standard deviations of the components so that they sum to one.

The effect of this procedure is that **components that genuinely discriminate within a candidate pool are automatically up-weighted, while components with almost no discriminating power are automatically down-weighted or driven to zero**. For example, if the thermal stability of all candidates in a direction clusters around 0.4, the component has no discriminating ability, its standard deviation is close to zero, and its weight automatically tends to zero; it does not occupy a fifth of the total merely because it was set to 0.2 by hand. Conversely, if cell penetration spans a wide range in that pool, its weight automatically rises. This is why the weights differ across the four directions — they are computed from the data, not configured.

The composite score actually uses six components: cell penetration, solubility, thermal stability, allergenicity (sign-flipped), B-cell epitope propensity (sign-flipped) and aggregation propensity (sign-flipped). The first three are modulated by the route's weight tier, whose values are **1.8× for high, 1.0× for medium and 0.4× for low**; the other three participate at 1.0×, retaining their data-determined contribution without introducing route differences. **When any component has no measured value, it drops out of the sum rather than counting as zero**, and the remaining weights are renormalized, so that the denominator is not distorted by missing measurements.

None of these six components is a functional-activity score, which is deliberate and is a principle established by the pipeline: **every metric is used exactly once, in one stage.** The functional score appears only in the ranking of the first round and is not referenced in any later round. This rule targets a failure mode that occurs easily — if the functional score kept participating in the later weighting, it would entirely mask the fitness of the scaffold and the route, and what got selected in the end would be "the peptide with the highest functional score" rather than "the peptide that performs best in the context of the current scaffold and route". These two are not the same thing, and the platform needs the latter. Another consequence of the same principle is that a candidate with an extremely high functional score is still eliminated if it fails the safety gates, receiving no compensation whatever for its outstanding activity.

There is one correction to the original pipeline implementation that should be recorded. The pipeline defines three tiers of delivery multipliers covering the cell-penetration, solubility and thermal-stability components, but in the actual calculation the multiplier is looked up by the key of the weight dictionary, and the keys of that dictionary are names that do not exist among the three multipliers; as a result **the multipliers for solubility and thermal stability never took effect**, only cell penetration actually received a multiplier, and the scores computed for the four delivery methods were therefore nearly identical. The server implementation retains the three modulated components of the original design intent and actually applies the multipliers by the route's weight tier, while the other three components participate in the weighting at 1.0×. This means that the composite score output by the server is not equal to the delivery scores already present in the library; the two are not on the same footing, and the old values are retained for reference.

So that this number can be re-checked, every response also returns all the normalized component values used by the composite score, the weight each component received, the multiplier each component received, and the size of the candidate pool on which normalization was based. Any composite score can be reproduced by hand from a single response, without re-querying the database.

## 8. Delivery Routes: Barrier Integrity Determines Immune Exposure

The five application routes are not five parallel labels; they are ordered by a single physical quantity: **how intact the interface barrier that the construct ultimately contacts is**. This quantity directly determines the likelihood that the immune system encounters the fusion protein, and therefore also how far the immunogenicity threshold should be tightened.

The four barrier states, from weakest to strongest, are: **entering tissue** (injectable filling; the barrier is bypassed entirely and the construct enters the dermis or subcutaneous tissue, the only one of the five routes that genuinely enters the body); **barrier breached** (wound dressing; applied to an open wound, in direct contact with the wound bed and tissue fluid); **barrier intact but occluded** (sheet mask; intact skin but used under occlusion, which raises stratum corneum permeability, increasing both penetration and sensitization risk); **barrier intact without occlusion** (film-forming application; spread into a film on the skin surface or left as a protein film, the construct essentially remaining outside the stratum corneum); and **non-living surface** (hair care; contact is with the already keratinized, dead hair shaft, with no blood vessels, lymphatics or immune cells, the construct never entering the body).

The immunogenicity threshold takes its value in this order: entering tissue and barrier breached are both **0.35**, the two intact-barrier routes are both **0.50**, and the non-living surface is relaxed to **0.60**. Wound dressing taking the same strict threshold as injection is a point that needs explicit explanation: the wound barrier is already broken and the construct is in direct contact with tissue fluid, so the magnitude of immune exposure is of the same order as injection, and treating it as intact skin would underestimate the risk.

The three modulated developability components are likewise derived from this physical picture rather than assigned arbitrarily. **Film-forming application** is used at ambient temperature and has the lowest thermal-stability requirement (0.4×); with neither a needle nor occlusion-driven enhancement, membrane crossing becomes the main bottleneck, so cell penetration has the highest requirement (1.8×); spreading into a film requires high solubility (1.8×). **Sheet mask** relies on occlusion to enhance penetration, so its penetration requirement is slightly lower than that of unoccluded film-forming application and takes the medium tier; film forming and impregnation processes require high solubility, taken at 1.8×. **Injectable filling** enters tissue directly, so penetration is no longer necessary (0.4×), and solubility and thermal stability take the medium tier. **Wound dressing** likewise does not need penetration (0.4×). **Hair care** is applied to the hair shaft, has the lowest penetration requirement (0.4×), and requires high solubility (1.8×) to support shampoo and conditioner formulations.

In addition to weights and thresholds, each route carries a set of **non-veto additional checks**, which are the process or safety requirements specific to that dosage form: wound dressing requires sterility, biocompatibility and endotoxin control; injectable filling additionally requires injectability and rheological properties, as well as in vivo safety; sheet mask requires irritation assessment under occlusion; film-forming application requires film-forming and spreading properties; hair care requires adhesion to the hair shaft, wash resistance, and no adverse effect on hair feel. These items are not currently in the data and constitute the list of experiments that must still be performed for that route; they are presented in the interface as notes rather than as determinations.

## 9. Parts Not Included in This Version

The full pipeline design has eight rounds; the product presents only the first four, and the last four all concern three-dimensional structure and are explicitly excluded from this version.

The reason for exclusion was measured, not a matter of convenience in scoping. The fifth round uses a folding-prediction model to generate a three-dimensional structure for each construct, the sixth computes solvent-accessible surface area and three-dimensional aggregation propensity on that structure, the seventh produces another composite score from the structure, and the eighth validates a small number of selected constructs with a higher-accuracy model. The problem lies in the fifth round: the silk-fibroin-type scaffold sequences in the library consist mainly of short "glycine–glycine–any residue" repeat units, and for such highly repetitive sequences lacking a natural homologous structural template, general folding models give consistently low confidence — the measured **mean local distance difference test value is about 0.42** (this metric characterizes the model's grasp of local structure at each residue position, with a maximum of 1.00, and values above 0.70 are usually regarded as reliable). Subjecting untrustworthy structures to the subsequent solvent-exposure and aggregation analyses yields numbers with no decision value.

The product therefore concentrates its resources on the first four rounds, making each candidate's sequence, itemized scoring basis and expression plan solid, rather than displaying a structure that cannot be used. One historical legacy related to this also needs clarification: the old interface contained two things both called "composite score", one being the third-round weighted score described above and the other being the seventh round's "forty percent solvent-accessible surface area plus forty percent aggregation-propensity correction plus twenty percent structural confidence". The latter depends on the outputs of the fifth and sixth rounds and is excluded along with the structure track; the composite score presented by the product refers only to the former.
